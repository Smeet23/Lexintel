COMPLAINT FOR PATENT INFRINGEMENT

Plaintiff NexGen Robotics, Inc. ("NexGen" or "Plaintiff"), by and through its undersigned counsel, files this Complaint against Defendant AutoMate Global Corp. ("AutoMate" or "Defendant") and alleges as follows:

I. NATURE OF THE ACTION

1. This is an action for patent infringement under the patent laws of the United States, 35 U.S.C. § 1 et seq. NexGen brings this action to stop AutoMate's unauthorized use of NexGen's patented robotic process automation technology and to recover damages for such infringement.

2. NexGen is the owner by assignment of United States Patent No. 10,456,789 (the "'789 Patent"), entitled "Adaptive Robotic Process Automation System with Machine Learning-Based Decision Engine," issued on October 22, 2019. A true and correct copy of the '789 Patent is attached hereto as Exhibit A.

3. NexGen is also the owner by assignment of United States Patent No. 11,123,456 (the "'456 Patent"), entitled "Method and System for Intelligent Workflow Orchestration in Robotic Process Automation," issued on September 14, 2021. A true and correct copy of the '456 Patent is attached hereto as Exhibit B.

II. THE PARTIES

4. Plaintiff NexGen Robotics, Inc. is a corporation organized and existing under the laws of the State of Delaware, with its principal place of business at 2800 Sand Hill Road, Suite 150, Menlo Park, California 94025. NexGen is a leading developer of robotic process automation (RPA) solutions for enterprise customers, with annual revenues exceeding $120 million.

5. Defendant AutoMate Global Corp. is a corporation organized and existing under the laws of the State of New York, with its principal place of business at 1 World Trade Center, Floor 72, New York, New York 10007. Upon information and belief, AutoMate develops and sells competing RPA software products under the brand name "AutoMate Studio" and "AutoMate Enterprise."

III. JURISDICTION AND VENUE

6. This Court has subject matter jurisdiction over this action pursuant to 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331 and 1338(a), as this case arises under the patent laws of the United States.

7. This Court has personal jurisdiction over AutoMate because AutoMate conducts substantial business in this District, has offices located in San Jose, California, maintains servers and data centers in this District, and has committed acts of patent infringement in this District.

8. Venue is proper in this District under 28 U.S.C. §§ 1391(b) and 1400(b) because AutoMate has committed acts of infringement in this District and has a regular and established place of business in this District.

IV. FACTUAL BACKGROUND

A. NexGen's Pioneering RPA Technology

9. NexGen was founded in 2014 by Dr. Maya Chen and Dr. Raj Krishnamurthy, both former researchers at Stanford University's Artificial Intelligence Laboratory. Since its founding, NexGen has invested over $85 million in research and development of its RPA technology platform.

10. NexGen's RPA platform, marketed under the name "NexFlow," is used by over 500 enterprise customers worldwide, including 42 Fortune 500 companies. The NexFlow platform enables businesses to automate repetitive tasks using software robots ("bots") that can interact with digital systems and software in the same way a human worker would.

11. A key innovation of NexGen's technology, protected by the '789 Patent, is the use of machine learning algorithms to enable RPA bots to make intelligent decisions during workflow execution. Unlike traditional RPA systems that follow rigid, pre-programmed rules, NexGen's patented technology allows bots to adapt to variations in input data and process flows by analyzing patterns in historical execution data.

12. The '456 Patent protects NexGen's innovative workflow orchestration system, which enables multiple RPA bots to coordinate their activities in real-time using a centralized decision engine. This system dynamically allocates tasks among bots based on workload, priority, and bot capability, resulting in significant efficiency gains.

B. AutoMate's Infringing Products

13. Upon information and belief, beginning no later than January 2023, AutoMate launched a product called "AutoMate Studio 5.0" that incorporates technology that infringes NexGen's patents. AutoMate Studio 5.0 includes a feature called "SmartBot Decision Engine" that uses machine learning to enable bots to make dynamic decisions during workflow execution.

14. AutoMate has also introduced "AutoMate Enterprise Orchestrator," a workflow coordination platform that dynamically allocates tasks among multiple bots based on workload analysis and priority scoring — functionality that directly infringes the claims of the '456 Patent.

15. NexGen first became aware of AutoMate's potentially infringing activities in March 2023, when NexGen's engineering team analyzed the technical documentation and marketing materials for AutoMate Studio 5.0 published on AutoMate's website.

16. On June 15, 2023, NexGen's outside patent counsel sent a detailed letter to AutoMate's General Counsel, identifying the '789 Patent and '456 Patent and explaining how AutoMate's products infringe these patents. The letter requested that AutoMate cease and desist from further infringement and enter into licensing negotiations.

17. AutoMate responded on July 28, 2023, through its outside counsel at Baker & Sterling LLP, denying any infringement and refusing to engage in licensing discussions. AutoMate's letter asserted, without substantive analysis, that its products do not infringe NexGen's patents and that the patents are invalid.

18. Despite NexGen's attempts to resolve this matter without litigation, AutoMate has continued to manufacture, use, sell, offer to sell, and import infringing products into the United States.

V. CLAIMS FOR RELIEF

COUNT I: INFRINGEMENT OF U.S. PATENT NO. 10,456,789

19. NexGen incorporates by reference the allegations of paragraphs 1 through 18 above.

20. The '789 Patent, entitled "Adaptive Robotic Process Automation System with Machine Learning-Based Decision Engine," was duly and legally issued by the United States Patent and Trademark Office on October 22, 2019. The '789 Patent is valid and enforceable.

21. NexGen is the owner by assignment of the '789 Patent and holds all rights, title, and interest in the '789 Patent, including the right to sue for past and future infringement.

22. AutoMate has directly infringed and continues to directly infringe at least Claims 1, 4, 7, and 12 of the '789 Patent by making, using, selling, offering to sell, and/or importing into the United States its AutoMate Studio 5.0 product and the SmartBot Decision Engine feature contained therein.

23. Claim 1 of the '789 Patent recites: "A robotic process automation system comprising: a process recording module configured to capture user interactions with one or more software applications; a machine learning engine trained on historical execution data to generate decision models; a bot execution module that executes automated workflows and dynamically selects execution paths based on the decision models; and a feedback loop that continuously updates the decision models based on execution outcomes."

24. AutoMate Studio 5.0's SmartBot Decision Engine practices each and every element of Claim 1. Specifically: (a) AutoMate Studio 5.0 includes a "Process Recorder" that captures user interactions; (b) it employs a machine learning system trained on workflow execution history to generate decision rules; (c) its bots dynamically select execution paths based on these learned decision rules; and (d) it includes a "Learning Loop" feature that updates decision models based on bot execution results.

25. AutoMate's infringement of the '789 Patent has been willful. AutoMate had actual knowledge of the '789 Patent no later than June 15, 2023, when NexGen sent its cease-and-desist letter, yet AutoMate continued to sell infringing products and refused to take a license.

COUNT II: INFRINGEMENT OF U.S. PATENT NO. 11,123,456

26. NexGen incorporates by reference the allegations of paragraphs 1 through 25 above.

27. The '456 Patent, entitled "Method and System for Intelligent Workflow Orchestration in Robotic Process Automation," was duly and legally issued by the United States Patent and Trademark Office on September 14, 2021. The '456 Patent is valid and enforceable.

28. AutoMate has directly infringed and continues to directly infringe at least Claims 1, 3, 5, and 8 of the '456 Patent by making, using, selling, offering to sell, and/or importing into the United States its AutoMate Enterprise Orchestrator product.

29. Claim 1 of the '456 Patent recites: "A method for orchestrating robotic process automation workflows comprising: receiving a plurality of workflow tasks from one or more task queues; analyzing each task to determine resource requirements and priority level; dynamically allocating tasks to available bot instances based on bot capability profiles and current workload; monitoring task execution in real-time; and reallocating tasks among bot instances when execution anomalies are detected."

30. AutoMate Enterprise Orchestrator practices each element of Claim 1, including its "Dynamic Task Router" feature that analyzes incoming tasks, its "Bot Pool Manager" that allocates tasks based on bot profiles and workload, and its "Anomaly Detector" that triggers task reallocation.

VI. DAMAGES

31. As a direct and proximate result of AutoMate's infringement of the '789 Patent and '456 Patent, NexGen has suffered and will continue to suffer substantial monetary damages.

32. Upon information and belief, AutoMate has generated revenues in excess of $200 million from sales of AutoMate Studio 5.0 and AutoMate Enterprise Orchestrator since their launch in January 2023. NexGen is entitled to damages adequate to compensate for AutoMate's infringement, which shall be no less than a reasonable royalty.

33. Based on NexGen's preliminary analysis, NexGen seeks damages of no less than $45,000,000, representing a reasonable royalty on AutoMate's infringing product revenues.

34. NexGen further seeks enhanced damages up to three times the amount found or assessed under 35 U.S.C. § 284, based on AutoMate's willful infringement of the '789 Patent.

35. NexGen also seeks pre-judgment and post-judgment interest on all damages awarded.

VII. PRAYER FOR RELIEF

WHEREFORE, Plaintiff NexGen Robotics, Inc. respectfully requests that this Court:

A. Enter judgment that AutoMate has infringed the '789 Patent and the '456 Patent;

B. Enter a permanent injunction enjoining AutoMate and its officers, agents, servants, employees, attorneys, and all persons in active concert or participation with them from further infringement of the '789 Patent and the '456 Patent;

C. Award NexGen damages adequate to compensate for AutoMate's infringement, in an amount no less than $45,000,000;

D. Award NexGen enhanced damages up to three times the amount of compensatory damages based on AutoMate's willful infringement, pursuant to 35 U.S.C. § 284;

E. Award NexGen its reasonable attorneys' fees and costs pursuant to 35 U.S.C. § 285;

F. Award NexGen pre-judgment and post-judgment interest; and

G. Grant such other and further relief as this Court deems just and proper.
